    Can response outrun consequence?

    Consequence means the earliest impact across mapped groups.

    Three legs, added together. To pass, even the slowest version of your response has to finish before the earliest version of the consequence. If those two overlap at all, it does not pass — an overlap means it could go either way, and that is not a margin.

    What do the “how do you know?” answers mean?

    This is the part most risk registers skip. A timing you believe is not the same as a timing you have watched happen. The ladder runs from weakest to strongest:

    1. Asserted in the room — someone said it just now. Fine to start with; not a basis for a decision.
    2. Documented — it is written in a plan or contract. Still nobody has done it.
    3. Technically verified — a system can demonstrably do its part. Says nothing about the humans.
    4. Exercised — you have rehearsed it end to end, deliberately.
    5. Observed in operation — it has actually happened, in real conditions.

    Favourable timing backed only by the first two returns “Timing works on paper.” That is not the tool being awkward — it is the difference between a plan and a capability, and it is usually the finding worth taking out of the room.

      Two scales, one rule

      Loss of control is not a size.
      It is a race, and it is always the same race.

      The frontier-safety literature and a council incident plan are usually treated as different subjects. They are the same inequality.       The method

      Risk registers list systems.
      Cascades live in the arrows.

      Civic Cascade makes one narrow claim testable: whether detection, decision authority, and fallback can finish before the earliest public consequence on every mapped service path.

      MAP

      Trace one complete path

      Start from one AI use. Name the service that inherits it and the people facing the earliest consequence.

      STRESS

      Put evidence on the clock

      Enter conservative time bands for detection, authority, fallback, and consequence—plus the basis for each claim.

      ACT

      Make a rule, not a score

      Response passes only when its slowest bound is strictly earlier than the first plausible consequence.

      Deliberate limit

      The result compares user-entered elapsed-time bands. Overlap is not called containment, unknowns fail closed, and a paper plan is not treated as exercised. The tool does not estimate likelihood or declare a system safe.
